# 10 Lightweight Android Apps That Won't Eat Your Storage

Modern apps have gotten bloated. A simple notes app can weigh 150MB. A calculator can demand camera and contacts permissions. If you're tired of storage warnings and mystery background processes, open-source apps are often the answer — smaller, faster, and honest about what they actually do.

Here are ten apps that do one thing well, weigh almost nothing, and respect your storage.

## Why Size Actually Matters

A smaller app usually means:

- **Fewer dependencies** — less code means fewer things that can go wrong or spy on you
- **Faster installs and updates** — especially useful on slower connections
- **Lower RAM usage** — your phone stays snappier with more apps installed
- **Easier to audit** — a 2MB app is far easier to review for safety than a 200MB one

## The List

### 1. Simple Calendar Pro
A clean calendar app with no ads, no account required, and full offline support. Handles recurring events, reminders, and widgets without demanding your Google account.

### 2. AntennaPod
A podcast player that's actually built for podcasts — not a media hub disguised as one. Download, queue, and listen offline with zero tracking.

### 3. NewPipe
Watch YouTube without an account, without ads, and without YouTube's tracking. Background playback and downloads included.

### 4. Aegis Authenticator
A two-factor authentication app that keeps your codes encrypted and backed up locally. No cloud dependency, no phone-home behavior.

### 5. Simple Gallery
A photo viewer and manager that doesn't upload your images anywhere. Fast thumbnail loading even on older hardware.

### 6. OpenTracks
GPS tracking for runs, rides, and hikes — all data stays on your device unless you choose to export it.

### 7. Markor
A markdown-based notes and to-do app. Plain text files you fully own, syncable with any tool you like (or none at all).

### 8. Amaze File Manager
A proper file manager without ads or "cleaner" upsells. Root support if you need it, none of the nagging if you don't.

### 9. Fossify Clock
Alarms, timers, and a stopwatch — nothing more, nothing less. No permissions it doesn't need.

### 10. Tachiyomi (Mihon fork)
A manga reader that's fully offline-capable once content is downloaded, with a clean and fast interface.

## How to Verify an App Is Actually Lightweight

Before installing anything, check:

- **APK size** — listed on the download page
- **Permissions requested** — a flashlight app asking for contacts is a red flag
- **Source availability** — open-source apps let you (or anyone) verify what the code does
- **Update frequency** — actively maintained apps tend to stay lean; abandoned ones often bloat before they die

## Final Thought

You don't need a 100MB app to check the weather or take notes. Smaller, well-built software is often the more trustworthy choice — not because size alone means quality, but because apps built to do one job well rarely need to be bloated in the first place.
